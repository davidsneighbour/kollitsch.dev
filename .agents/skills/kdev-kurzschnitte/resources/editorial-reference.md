# Kurzschnitte editorial reference

## Audience

Write for experienced or aspiring web developers, designers, technical
generalists, independent makers, and curious readers who enjoy useful corners of
the web. Assume technical literacy without assuming specialist knowledge in every
subfield.

## Voice

- British English.
- Direct, informed, and lightly conversational.
- Dry humour is welcome.
- Mild scepticism is preferable to breathless enthusiasm.
- Avoid corporate marketing language.
- Explain why something is interesting, not merely what its landing page claims.

## Description pattern

A strong item usually contains:

1. context or problem;
2. the linked resource;
3. its practical or intellectual value;
4. one memorable detail or restrained editorial observation.

Example shape:

```markdown
- Sticky interfaces are easy to add and surprisingly difficult to make pleasant.
  [Resource title](https://example.com/) examines the awkward cases rather than
  repeating the basic recipe, with enough implementation detail to be useful when
  a header must change behaviour without turning scrolling into an event-listener
  endurance test.
```

Do not copy the example into posts.

## Categorisation test

Ask: "Why would the reader open this?"

- To implement or debug a website: `WebDev`
- To improve visual work or systems: `Design`
- To use a practical utility: `Tools`
- To understand a subject: `Learn`
- To improve inclusive implementation: `Accessibility`
- To reduce risk or surveillance: `Privacy and security`
- To consider an argument or wider consequence: `Food for thought`
- To enjoy an oddity, game, experiment, or joke: `Fun and stuff`
- To covet an expensive or futuristic object: `FOMO`
- To consume video: `Watch`
- To consume substantial written work: `Read`
- To consume audio: `Listen`

Use the resource's primary value, not every possible secondary use.
